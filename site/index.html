One key, in any app.

Hushflow stays out of sight until you need it. Put your cursor in an email, a doc, a chat or a code editor, and talk. There's no window to switch to.

  1. fn CtrlWin

    Hold to talk

    Press and hold, say what you want to write, then let go. Your words land at the cursor.

  2. fn CtrlWin tap

    Tap for hands-free

    For longer thoughts, tap instead of holding. It keeps listening until you tap again.

  3. esc

    Changed your mind?

    Press Esc to cancel. Nothing gets typed.

  4. ctrl⌘V AltShiftZ

    Paste it again

    Clicked away too soon? Paste your last dictation again, wherever you are now.

Every shortcut can be remapped in Settings.

Talk the way you think. Read it the way you'd write it.

Turn on Clean up my text and a small language model on your computer tidies each dictation before it's typed. It keeps your words and your meaning, and drops the noise.

Um, so I think, I think I think we should like push the launch uh a week.

So I think we should push the launch a week.

Fillers and false starts

The ums, the likes, the sentence you started twice. They're gone before the text appears.

Let's meet Monday, no wait, Tuesday at ten.

Let's meet Tuesday at ten.

Corrections you make out loud

Say "no wait" and carry on. Hushflow keeps the version you settled on.

Shopping list uh eggs, spinach, oat milk and um coffee beans

Shopping list:

  • Eggs
  • Spinach
  • Oat milk
  • Coffee beans

What you say stays on your computer.

Hushflow turns speech into text and cleans it up on your own machine. Your voice and your words aren't sent to a server to be transcribed or rewritten. And because the code is open, you don't have to take that on trust.

  • Local speech recognition
  • Local cleanup model
  • Open source under MIT

Ready the moment you install it.

On macOS 26 and later, Hushflow starts with Apple's built-in speech recognizer, so there's nothing to wait for. Meanwhile it downloads NVIDIA's Parakeet model in the background and switches over once it's ready. Prefer one engine? Pick it yourself.

Questions, answered.

Is Hushflow really free?

Yes. It's open source under the MIT license. There's no subscription and no paid tier.

Does anything I say leave my computer?

No. Speech recognition runs locally (Apple's built-in recognizer or Parakeet), and so does the cleanup model. The models are downloaded once; your audio and text are processed on your machine.

Which apps does it work in?

Any app where you can type. Hushflow puts the text wherever your cursor is: email, chat, documents, notes, the browser or your code editor.

What languages does it support?

English only for now.

What do I need on a Mac?

The current builds are for Apple Silicon Macs. On macOS 26 or later, Hushflow can start with Apple's built-in speech recognizer straight away, while Parakeet downloads in the background. On older versions it uses Parakeet once it's downloaded.

Why does my computer warn me when I open it?

The builds aren't code-signed yet. On a Mac, right-click the app and choose Open (or run the xattr command above). On Windows, choose More info, then Run anyway.

What does "Clean up my text" actually change?

It removes filler words and false starts, applies corrections you make out loud ("Monday, no wait, Tuesday" becomes "Tuesday"), and formats lists and paragraphs. It doesn't answer questions or follow instructions in what you dictate; if the model tries to, Hushflow pastes your original words instead. You can turn it off at any time.

Can I change the shortcuts?

Yes. Hold-to-talk, hands-free, cancel and paste-last can all be remapped in Settings.

How is it related to Handy?

Hushflow is a fork of Handy, the open-source dictation app by CJ Pais, and it tracks upstream. It adds hold-or-tap shortcuts, an instant-start speech engine on Mac and the local cleanup model.
